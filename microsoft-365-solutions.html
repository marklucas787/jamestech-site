SharePoint, Teams, Power Automate, Power Apps, Copilot. I set them up around how your firm actually works, clean up the tenant, and train your people to run it. Columbia, Maryland.

By Mark Lucas, founder of JamesTech LLC, Columbia, MD. Roughly thirty years on the Microsoft stack, three of them at Microsoft. Artemis program contractor at NASA.

The problem

Your firm runs on Microsoft 365. It probably wasn't set up for it.

Email moved to Exchange years ago and everything else stayed where it was. The files are on an office server nobody can reach from home, or spread across personal OneDrives. Teams got turned on and never adopted. Copilot got bought for everyone and opened by two people.

Here's what I find in most 5 to 200 person tenants on the first look:

  • →Seats still paid for people who left last year, and one shared admin login everybody knows.
  • →Permissions granted to individuals one file at a time, so nobody can say who can see what.
  • →The same procedure in three documents in three places, all slightly different.
  • →Recurring work done by hand, re-keying a form into a spreadsheet into an email, that Power Automate could have been doing since 2019.
  • →Licenses for tools nobody has opened. Business Premium, E3, Copilot, Visio, Project. Paid monthly, used never.

None of that is a security product problem. It's a setup problem, and setup is the part I do.

Assess, build, hand over.

  • 01Assess. I read the tenant and sit with the people who use it. You get a plain-English list of what you own, what you use, what's exposed, and what to build first, ranked by the hours it gives back.

The work

What I do inside Microsoft 365.

SharePoint and document management

Sites and libraries that match how the firm is organized. Metadata instead of nine levels of folders. Permissions by group, not by person. The office server retired, with OneDrive syncing the new structure to every computer.

Teams, set up to be used

A few teams and channels that map to real work, not one per whim. Meeting recordings and transcripts landing where people can find them. Guest access decided on purpose.

Power Automate workflows

Approvals, reminders, document routing, status updates, and the hand-offs between systems (payroll, accounting, CRM, forms). One client's 40-hour manual task now runs in minutes.

Power Apps and Dataverse

The tracker that's currently a spreadsheet with six tabs, rebuilt as an app with a proper table behind it, row-level history, and a dashboard for whoever asks for the numbers.

Copilot and Copilot Studio

Copilot for the seats where it changes the work, priced at the real per-seat cost, after the documents are organized enough for it to be useful. Copilot Studio agents that answer from your own SharePoint content under your own permissions.

Tenant housekeeping

Licensing review, idle seats removed, admin roles named, groups instead of one-off grants, guest accounts audited, retention set. Every change requested through your IT provider, one setting at a time, and documented.

The line

Where your IT provider fits.

Keep them. Logins, MFA, passkeys, devices, network, backups, and security monitoring are theirs, and they should stay theirs. I build and run what your staff use on top of that layer, and when I need a tenant setting changed, they get a one-line ticket with the exact record.

The same division holds for AI. Once the documents are organized and the permissions make sense, the agents that read them need an owner too. That's the agentic AI management side of the practice, and this page is usually what comes first.

The cost of waiting

What happens if nothing changes.

The tenant keeps getting messier, because every new hire adds a folder and every departure leaves a seat. The person who knows where everything is keeps being the only person who knows. The manual work keeps costing the same hours every week, and the Copilot seats keep billing whether anyone opens them or not.

Then somebody asks the AI a question about the business and it answers like a stranger who met you five seconds ago, because the knowledge it needed was in a drawer, an inbox, and one employee's head. That's the moment most firms call.

Questions

The honest answers.

What does a Microsoft 365 consultant do for a small firm? +

Sets up the parts of Microsoft 365 you already pay for around how the firm actually works: SharePoint for documents, Teams for communication, Power Automate for the recurring workflows, Power Apps for the forms and trackers, and Copilot where it earns its seat. I also clean up the tenant and train the team to run it.

Do you handle security, backups, and devices? +

No. Your IT provider keeps logins, MFA, devices, network, backups, and security monitoring. I build and run the workloads on top of that layer and request any tenant change through them, one setting at a time.

Our files are still on an office server. Can you move them? +

Yes. A messy file server moved to the cloud is still a messy file server, so the move starts with a structure: which sites, which libraries, who owns each one, what permissions apply. Then the files move in, and OneDrive syncs them to every computer so staff keep working the way they did.

Is Copilot worth it for a 10 person firm? +

Sometimes, for some seats. Copilot works well when the documents are organized and the permissions are right, and poorly when they aren't. I price it at the real per-seat cost, recommend it for the people whose work it will change, and say so when it won't pay for itself.
