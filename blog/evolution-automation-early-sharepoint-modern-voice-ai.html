The Evolution of Automation: From Early SharePoint to Modern Voice AI

By Mark Lucas, founder of JamesTech LLC, Columbia, MD. Roughly thirty years on the Microsoft stack, three of them at Microsoft.

# The Evolution of Automation: From Early SharePoint to Modern Voice AI !A conceptual timeline of business automation, starting from a vintage beige 90s computer monitor on the left, transitioning through a glowing blue digital cloud in the middle, and ending with a sleek, minimalist holographic interface representing Voice AI on the right. The lighting is warm and natural, emphasizing a journey from hardware to intelligent software. Photorealistic style, shallow depth of field. I’ve spent more than 30 years in the "engine room" of business technology. I’ve seen the rise and fall of dozens of "next big things," but one truth has remained constant: the goal of technology is: and always should be: to make work easier. When I first started, "automation" meant writing complex scripts that lived on a local server and maybe, if you were lucky, sent an automated email when a file was moved. Today, we’re building Voice AI agents that can hold full conversations, reason through complex problems, and trigger entire business workflows without a single human click. The evolution of business automation isn't just about the tools getting faster or shinier; it’s about the shift from deterministic systems: where we had to tell the computer exactly what to do: to agentic systems that understand what we’re trying to achieve. ## The Early Days: The "Rigid" Rules of SharePoint Back in the early 2000s, SharePoint was the king of the castle. For those of us building intranets and document management systems, it was a revolution. Before SharePoint, if you wanted to route a document for approval, you were likely literally walking a piece of paper to someone’s desk or hoping an email didn't get lost in a cluttered inbox. SharePoint introduced us to the concept of the "workflow." But let’s be honest: those early workflows were rigid. They were strictly "If-Then" logic. If a user uploads a file to Folder A... Then send an email to Manager B. If Manager B clicks "Approve"... Then move the file to Folder C. It was powerful, but it was fragile. If Manager B was on vacation or if someone uploaded a PDF instead of a Word doc, the whole process ground to a halt. We were digitizing bureaucracy, not necessarily eliminating it. At JamesTech LLC, I often look back at those days to remind myself how far we’ve come. We were building digital filing cabinets. They were better than physical ones, but they still required a human to do all the thinking. !A person working in a bright, modern office using a laptop and tablet, demonstrating a flexible, tech-enabled workspace. This represents the shift from rigid physical filing to the early days of digital collaboration. ## The Cloud Shift: Connectivity is Everything The real turning point in the evolution of business automation came with the shift to the cloud and the birth of Microsoft 365. Suddenly, SharePoint wasn't just a silo on a server in the basement; it was part of an interconnected ecosystem. This is where Power Automate (formerly Microsoft Flow) changed the game. For the first time, we could connect SharePoint to things outside of Microsoft. We could connect it to Google Workspace, to HubSpot, or to custom APIs. I’ve always focused on helping non-profits and small businesses navigate this. Many of these teams don’t have a massive IT budget, but they do have massive workloads. By using the Microsoft Power Platform, we started moving away from "folders" and toward "processes." Instead of just storing a document, the system could now read the document. We began using AI Builder to extract data from invoices and automatically update a spreadsheet. The automation became "connected," but it was still mostly following a map we had to draw for it. !A stylized Microsoft Power Platform icon centered within a translucent sphere, featuring hexagonal patterns to emphasize cloud integration and modern data workflows. ## The Intelligence Explosion: Copilot and n8n In the last few years, we hit the "Intelligence" phase. With the introduction of Large Language Models (LLMs) and tools like Microsoft 365 Copilot, the computer stopped being a passive tool and started becoming a collaborator. This changed how I approach my work at Velocity Voice AI. It’s no longer just about moving data from Point A to Point B. It’s about context. Take a modern non-profit. They might receive hundreds of grant applications. In the old days, a human had to read every single one to see if it met the criteria. Then, we built automations that looked for keywords. Today? We use AI to "read" the application, summarize the key points, and even draft a response based on the organization's unique voice. I’m also a huge advocate for n8n, an open-source automation tool that allows for incredibly complex, logic-heavy workflows that are much more flexible than traditional "low-code" platforms. It allows us to bridge the gap between Microsoft 365 and the rest of the web in a way that feels seamless. !Three professionals collaborate around a laptop, interacting with a futuristic digital dashboard. The scene emphasizes teamwork and data-driven insights with AI-powered automation. ## The Current Frontier: Modern Voice AI And that brings us to where I am focusing my energy now: Voice AI. If you had told me 20 years ago that I’d be building AI agents that can answer a phone call, understand a customer's frustration, look up their record in a CRM, and solve their problem: all in a natural-sounding voice: I might have called it science fiction. But it’s here. And it’s the ultimate expression of business automation. Voice AI isn't just a "chatbot that talks." It is a sophisticated Agentic AI. It doesn't just follow a script; it follows a goal. * Old Automation: "If the user says 'Billing,' route the call to the billing department." * Modern Voice AI: "The user is calling about an overcharge. Access their last three invoices, identify the discrepancy, explain it to them, and offer a credit if it falls under our $50 policy." For a small business owner, this is a literal life-changer. It means you don't need a 24/7 call center to provide 24/7 service. You can have a "digital twin" of your best customer service rep available at all times. This is why I rebranded as Velocity Voice AI: because voice is the most natural interface humans have, and we finally have the tech to support it. !A close-up of a person speaking into a sleek, modern smartphone. From the screen, a series of glowing, rhythmic sound waves emerge, symbolizing the fluid and natural interaction of modern Voice AI. Professional photography, bright and clean aesthetic. ## Why Experience Matters in the Age of AI You might wonder why a guy with 30 years of "old school" tech experience is so excited about AI. It’s because I’ve seen what happens when tech is implemented for tech’s sake. I’ve seen the "over-engineered" SharePoint sites that no one uses and the complex automations that break the moment a password is changed. The evolution of business automation has taught me that the "how" matters less than the "why." Whether I’m helping a non-profit set up a simple SharePoint intranet or architecting a complex Voice AI agent, my focus is always on: 1. Affordability: You shouldn't need a Fortune 500 budget to have world-class tools. 2. Simplicity: If your team can’t use it, it’s not an "improvement." 3. Measurable Value: Does this actually give you an hour of your day back? ## Looking Ahead The tools will keep changing. In five years, we might be talking about neural interfaces or something even more "out there." But the journey from those early, clunky SharePoint workflows to the smooth, intelligent Voice AI agents of today proves one thing: we are getting better at letting computers be computers so that humans can be humans. If you’re still feeling stuck in the "rigid rules" era of automation, or if you’re curious about how Voice AI can help your team reclaim their time, let’s talk.
