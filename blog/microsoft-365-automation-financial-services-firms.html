Microsoft 365 Automation for Financial Services Firms

By Mark Lucas, founder of JamesTech LLC, Columbia, MD. Roughly thirty years on the Microsoft stack, three of them at Microsoft.

# Microsoft 365 Automation for Financial Services Firms !A diverse financial-services team collaborating around a laptop with secure workflow and approval dashboards Financial-services firms are under constant pressure to move faster while protecting sensitive information. Advisors need timely access to client records. Operations teams need consistent processes. Compliance teams need evidence that reviews happened, approvals were documented, and policies were followed. For many RIAs, wealth managers, small banks, and insurance agencies, the answer is not necessarily another enterprise platform. If your firm already uses Microsoft 365, you may have the foundation for a secure, audit-ready operating environment already in place. Exchange, SharePoint, Teams, OneDrive, Power Platform, and Copilot can work together to manage documents, coordinate reviews, automate approvals, and reduce repetitive administrative work. The important qualification is that Microsoft 365 does not create compliance automatically. Your firm still needs appropriate policies, configuration, licensing, access controls, training, and regular review. But with the right design, Microsoft 365 can support practical workflow automation for small business without requiring a major software replacement. ## Start with the Microsoft 365 tools you already own A typical financial-services firm may use Outlook for email, OneDrive for files, Teams for meetings, and SharePoint somewhere in the background. The problem is often not a lack of tools. It is that each tool is being used separately. A better approach is to define the role of each service: - Exchange and Outlook: communication, shared mailboxes, alerts, and controlled email retention - SharePoint: the controlled document hub and system of record - Teams: governed collaboration spaces for departments, cases, and client work - OneDrive: individual working files that are moved to SharePoint when finalized - Power Automate: approvals, notifications, reminders, escalations, and reporting - Power Apps: structured intake forms and internal business applications - Copilot: drafting, summarizing, searching, and preparing information for human review Microsoft’s SharePoint guidance for financial services describes many of these capabilities, including document versioning, metadata, information barriers, retention policies, workflow alerts, and approval processes. The goal is not to turn every process into a complicated application. It is to create a clear path for information from intake to review, approval, storage, and reporting. ## 1. Use Exchange for secure, manageable email Email remains central to financial services. Client requests, policy notices, approval questions, regulatory communications, and supporting documents often arrive through Outlook. That makes email an important part of your firm’s operational controls. A practical design might use: 1. A shared mailbox for a defined process, such as onboarding, compliance requests, or policy exceptions. 2. Rules or Power Automate triggers that identify specific messages. 3. A structured SharePoint record created from the email. 4. Tasks assigned to the appropriate reviewer. 5. Notifications sent to Teams or Outlook. 6. A documented resolution and retention policy. For example, when an onboarding request arrives in a designated mailbox, Power Automate could create an item in a SharePoint list, save approved attachments to the correct document library, assign an operations task, and notify a compliance reviewer. The flow should not give every participant broad access to the mailbox or document library. Instead, access should be based on role, process stage, and business need. Email retention also requires careful planning. Decide which communications must be retained, for how long, and whether they need a legal hold or other special treatment. Microsoft Purview can support retention, sensitivity labels, data loss prevention, and audit capabilities, but the correct configuration depends on your regulatory obligations and Microsoft licensing. ## 2. Govern Teams instead of letting it become a second filing system Teams is excellent for collaboration, but unmanaged Teams environments can create their own version of “permission hell.” A new Team creates connected SharePoint storage. If teams are created without naming standards, owners, expiration rules, or membership reviews, sensitive information can become difficult to manage. For financial-services firms, consider templates for recurring workspaces such as: - Client onboarding - Lending or underwriting - Compliance reviews - Insurance claims - Investment committee work - Operations and service requests Each template can include standard channels, a SharePoint document library, a Power App, a Planner board, and the Approvals app. Governance should define: - Who can create a Team - Who owns it - Whether guests are permitted - Which channels may contain confidential information - How often membership is reviewed - When inactive teams are archived - Where final records must be stored - Which conversations belong in a system of record Teams should support work, not replace formal records management. A decision made in chat may need to be captured in a controlled approval record or document. Power Automate can help by creating structured records from forms, approvals, or defined workflow events. Microsoft’s financial-services Teams documentation highlights scenarios such as simplifying business processes, using Approvals, connecting SharePoint, and tracking key performance indicators with Power BI. ## 4. Automate approvals, reminders, and reporting with Power Automate Many financial-services workflows follow predictable steps: 1. Someone submits a request. 2. Operations verifies the information. 3. A manager reviews it. 4. Compliance or risk provides an additional check. 5. The decision is recorded. 6. The requester is notified. 7. Evidence is retained for future review. Power Automate can coordinate these steps without requiring staff to manually send reminders or update multiple spreadsheets. Useful starting points include: - New account or client onboarding - Policy exception requests - Marketing and communication approvals - Vendor reviews - Document expiration monitoring - Compliance attestations - Credit or underwriting approvals - Periodic access reviews - Management reporting A well-designed flow should record more than “approved” or “rejected.” It should capture the request, decision, reviewer, timestamp, comments, supporting documents, and any escalation. That creates a more useful audit trail and makes reporting easier. A SharePoint list or Dataverse table can provide the structured data behind dashboards showing: - Open requests - Aging items - Average approval time - Exceptions by category - Overdue reviews - Completion by department - Documents approaching expiration Start with standard connectors where possible. Premium connectors, Dataverse, custom APIs, and third-party integrations can be valuable, but they may affect licensing and implementation costs. An affordable automation is often better than an impressive solution that the firm cannot maintain. ## 5. Use Copilot for drafting—not unsupervised decisions Microsoft 365 Copilot can help financial professionals save time in Outlook, Word, Excel, and Teams. Appropriate uses may include: - Drafting a client follow-up email -...AI for financial services overview describes potential benefits such as workflow automation, improved analysis, risk support, and faster access to information. The key is to treat Copilot as an assistant, not an autonomous decision-maker. A human should review client communications, financial analysis, compliance interpretations, and recommendations before anything is sent or relied upon. Copilot also respects the permissions already present in Microsoft 365. That means poor permissions can create poor AI results. If users can access documents they should not see, an AI assistant may make those documents easier to discover. Permission cleanup and information architecture should come before broad AI deployment. ## Compliance depends on controls, not product names Financial-services firms should evaluate Microsoft 365 against their specific obligations and risk environment. Important design principles include: - Least privilege: users receive only the access required for their role - Separation of duties: the person submitting a request is not always the person approving it - Retention: documents and messages are retained according to policy - Auditability: actions, changes, approvals, and access can be reviewed - Data protection: sensitivity labels, encryption, and DLP are applied where appropriate - Governance: Teams, SharePoint sites, apps, and automations have owners - Human oversight: AI-generated content is reviewed before use - Licensing awareness: features are implemented only when the firm has the necessary licenses Microsoft provides additional information about Microsoft 365 security and compliance for financial services, including audit logging, information protection, data residency, encryption, and Teams capabilities. No platform eliminates the need for legal, compliance, or cybersecurity advice. Microsoft 365 is a set of capabilities that must be configured and operated responsibly. ## Review your licensing before buying more software Before adding a new system, review what your current Microsoft 365 plan already includes. Business Premium, Enterprise plans, Power Automate, Power Apps, Power BI, Purview, and Copilot each have different capabilities and licensing requirements. Some connectors and advanced features may require additional licenses. A sensible sequence is: 1. Inventory your current licenses. 2. Identify the processes causing the most risk or wasted time. 3. Determine which standard capabilities can address them. 4. Test the workflow with a small group. 5. Measure time saved, completion rates, and control improvements. 6. Add premium capabilities only when the value is clear. This approach is especially useful for firms that need practical IT solutions for small business rather than a large, expensive transformation program. ## Build a better Microsoft 365 operating model Microsoft 365 can support a secure and efficient financial-services operation, but successful automation starts with process clarity. If your firm is spending too much time searching for documents, chasing approvals, reconciling spreadsheets, or preparing routine reports, the next step is not necessarily a new platform. It may be a better structure for the tools you already have.
