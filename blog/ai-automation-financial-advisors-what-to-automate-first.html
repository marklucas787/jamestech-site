AI Automation for Financial Advisors: What to Automate First

By Mark Lucas, founder of JamesTech LLC, Columbia, MD. Roughly thirty years on the Microsoft stack, three of them at Microsoft.

# AI Automation for Financial Advisors: What to Automate First !A financial advisory team reviewing an organized client-intake workflow on laptops and tablets For financial advisors, registered investment advisers (RIAs), and small financial-services firms, AI automation is most valuable when it removes repetitive administrative work without removing professional judgment. That distinction matters. An AI system can organize information, identify missing fields, draft a follow-up, or prepare a meeting packet. It should not independently decide whether an investment is suitable, make a discretionary recommendation, or send client-facing advice without review. The best starting point is not “How can we automate everything?” It is: > Which repeatable steps consume time, follow a consistent pattern, and still have a clear human approval point? For most advisory firms, the answer is client intake, meeting documentation, form filling, routine follow-ups, and report preparation. This is where financial services workflow automation can create measurable value using tools such as Microsoft 365, Power Platform, AI Builder, and n8n. ## A practical rule: automate preparation, not judgment Advisors spend much of their day moving information between systems: - Copying client details from an intake form into a CRM - Searching for documents before a review meeting - Turning notes into tasks and follow-up emails - Re-entering information into custodian forms - Compiling portfolio, planning, and household information into a prep packet - Chasing missing signatures and documents These activities are structured and repeatable. They are good candidates for workflow automation for small business. By contrast, the following should remain human-led: - Investment recommendations - Suitability and best-interest determinations - Interpretation of a client’s goals and circumstances - Relationship decisions - Exceptions and unusual cases - Final approval of client-facing communications - Any discretionary action involving client assets The goal is not to replace the advisor. It is to give the advisor better information, fewer administrative tasks, and more time for client relationships. ## 1. Start with client intake and onboarding Client intake is usually the highest-value first automation because it touches multiple systems and creates downstream work. A typical manual process may look like this: 1. A prospective client completes a form. 2. An employee reviews it for missing information. 3. Data is entered into the CRM. 4. Documents are prepared manually. 5. The advisor sends follow-up questions. 6. Compliance checks are tracked in a spreadsheet. 7. Signed documents are saved to a folder. 8. The CRM and internal checklist are updated again. A better workflow can connect these steps. A Power Apps or Microsoft Forms intake experience can collect structured information. Power Automate can validate required fields, create a review task, and store submissions in SharePoint or Dataverse. AI Builder can extract information from documents such as PDFs, statements, or completed forms, subject to appropriate security and review controls. For firms that need to connect external systems, n8n can orchestrate the handoffs between a form provider, CRM, e-signature platform, document system, and approved AI service. ### Example intake workflow - A prospect submits an initial questionnaire. - The workflow checks for missing required fields. - A CRM record is created or updated. - A checklist is generated for the advisor or operations team. - Approved templates are populated with known information. - Documents are routed for signature. - Completed files are stored in the correct SharePoint library. - Exceptions, such as inconsistent addresses or incomplete ownership information, are routed to a human reviewer. - The system records who reviewed each step and when. The automation should make the process more consistent, not make compliance invisible. Every firm needs to define its own requirements for KYC, AML, privacy, recordkeeping, supervision, and client communications. ## 2. Automate meeting notes and task creation Meeting documentation is another strong candidate for AI for small business automation because advisors already create the source material. The workflow can begin with notes typed into OneNote, a Power App, a CRM, or a structured Microsoft Teams process. An approved AI step can then convert those notes into a consistent internal summary: - Topics discussed - Questions requiring follow-up - Documents requested - Changes in personal or financial circumstances - Tasks assigned to team members - Items requiring advisor or compliance review The output should be a draft, not an official interpretation of the conversation. For example, the system might identify: - “Client requested a beneficiary review” - “Send updated risk questionnaire” - “Schedule estate-planning discussion” - “Confirm transfer paperwork status” Power Automate can turn these into Planner tasks, CRM activities, or Outlook reminders. The advisor can correct, approve, or delete each item before it becomes part of the client record. !A financial professional reviewing paper documents and a digital device during a structured workflow ### What AI should not do with meeting notes Meeting notes may contain sensitive personal and financial information. Avoid sending them to an unapproved consumer AI tool or using a model without understanding its data handling, retention, access, and audit controls. The system should also avoid turning ambiguous comments into definitive client instructions. If the notes say, “Client is thinking about retirement in a few years,” the automation should preserve that uncertainty rather than convert it into a firm retirement date. ## 3. Use AI Builder for document and form preparation Form filling is one of the clearest examples of administrative work that can be accelerated without automating advice. Many advisory firms re-enter the same client information across: - Internal onboarding checklists - Advisory agreements - Custodian forms - Transfer paperwork - Risk questionnaires - Beneficiary documentation - Privacy and disclosure records AI Builder can help extract fields from supported document types, while Power Automate can move validated information through an approval process. A Power App can provide a review screen where staff confirm extracted values before anything is written to a system of record. A practical design uses confidence thresholds: - High-confidence fields can be presented for quick confirmation. - Low-confidence fields are flagged for manual entry. - Sensitive fields require explicit review. - Conflicting values stop the workflow rather than get silently overwritten. n8n can be useful when the process spans systems outside Microsoft 365. For example, it can receive a completed intake event, map approved fields to a CRM, call an e-signature service, monitor signature status, and send the completion event back to Power Automate. The important principle is simple: AI may suggest or extract a value; a person remains accountable for confirming it. ## 4. Automate routine follow-ups, with guardrails Follow-up communication is often repetitive but still important to the client experience. Examples include: - Welcome messages - Appointment reminders - Requests for missing documents - Review meeting confirmations - Notifications that a form is ready - Reminders about incomplete signatures - Internal alerts when a client has not responded These messages can be triggered by events in the CRM or document system. Power Automate can manage Microsoft 365-based notifications, while n8n can connect external applications. AI can help personalize a draft by referencing approved information, such as the client’s meeting date or the specific document requested. However, the content should remain within defined boundaries. A safe workflow might require that: 1. The AI uses an approved template. 2. The prompt prohibits recommendations, performance claims, guarantees, and unsupported facts. 3. The draft is saved for review rather than sent automatically. 4. The advisor approves the message. 5. Compliance review is added when required by firm policy. 6. The final communication is archived with the appropriate metadata. FINRA emphasizes that its rules are technology-neutral and continue to apply when firms use generative AI or third-party AI features. Its AI guidance and resources are a useful starting point for firms that serve FINRA-regulated clients. FINRA also directs firms to consider applicable securities laws and regulatory obligations when adopting new technologies. For RIAs and other firms outside FINRA membership, the exact obligations may differ. The workflow still needs to reflect the firm’s regulatory environment, policies, vendor oversight process, and recordkeeping requirements. ## 5. Automate meeting prep and report preparation Meeting preparation can consume hours because the relevant information is spread across multiple locations. A review meeting packet might require: - Recent portfolio information - CRM notes - Previous meeting action items - Open service requests - Planning assumptions - Missing documents - Recent client correspondence - Approved market or strategy commentary Automation can gather this information into a consistent internal packet. Power Automate can trigger the process a set number of days before a meeting. SharePoint can provide a controlled document location. n8n can connect portfolio, planning, or CRM systems that do not have a native Microsoft connector. AI is useful for organizing and summarizing the material: - “What changed since the last meeting?” - “Which tasks are still open?” - “Which documents are missing?” - “What questions did the client previously ask?” - “Which data points need advisor confirmation?” The portfolio system or source-of-record application should remain responsible for calculations and numerical accuracy. AI should not invent performance figures or independently interpret incomplete data. !A modern advisory team collaborating across laptops and tablets in a bright office For client-facing reports, use a stricter process. Automated narratives should be based on approved language and verified data. An advisor should review the content, and compliance should review it when required. The final report should be stored in the firm’s approved system with a clear audit trail. ## Build the controls before expanding the automation Compliance-aware automation is not just a matter of adding an approval button at the end. Controls should be designed into the workflow from the beginning. Consider including: - Role-based access to client and household data - Approved data sources and AI services - Required-field validation - Exception queues for unusual cases - Human approval before client-facing communication - Version history for documents and drafts - Timestamps and user identities for major actions - Retention and deletion rules - Vendor and connector reviews - Testing with sample or redacted data - A process for correcting AI-generated errors SharePoint can support document libraries, permissions, version history, and organized records. Power Platform can provide forms, approvals, notifications, and operational dashboards. n8n can connect the systems that sit outside Microsoft 365. The right architecture depends on your current CRM, e-signature provider, portfolio tools, data policies, and licensing. The best solution is usually the smallest one that reliably removes a meaningful bottleneck. ## What to automate first: a simple priority test Before selecting a tool, score each workflow against five questions: 1. Does the task happen frequently? 2. Does it follow a repeatable process? 3. Is the input information reasonably structured? 4. Can a person review the output before a consequential action? 5. Can success be measured in time saved, errors avoided, or faster service? If the answer is “yes” to most of these questions, the workflow is a strong candidate. For many financial advisors, the recommended order is: 1. Client intake and missing-information checks 2. Meeting note summaries and task creation 3. Document extraction and form preparation 4. Routine follow-up drafts and reminders 5. Meeting preparation packets and report assembly Keep investment advice, relationship decisions, suitability judgments, and discretionary actions human-led. ## A sensible next step for your firm You do not need a large transformation project to begin. Start with one workflow, document the current process, identify the approval points, and measure the result. An AI Opportunity Review or 90-minute audit can help identify where automation is likely to produce real value: and where it could introduce unnecessary risk. The review should examine your current Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace setup, CRM, forms, document libraries, recurring administrative tasks, and compliance workflow. The objective is not to add AI because it is fashionable. It is to make the right parts of your operation faster, clearer, and easier to supervise: so your team can spend more time doing the work only experienced financial professionals can do.
